Privacy Policy

The Quick Takeaway

We don't sell your data, track your location, or show you ads. Your medical info stays on your phone and on your wristband, plus a signed-in wearer copy only if you sign in and turn on account sync. Anyone who taps your band can read what you put on it—just like an old-school engraved metal medical bracelet. In a real emergency, always call 911 first.

1. Who We Are

RedMed is operated in New Jersey, USA. If you have any questions or need help with your order, email us directly at Admin@RedMed.live.

2. What We Keep (and What Stays Private)

  • Your Emergency Profile: You choose what to list—such as your name, blood type, allergies, daily medications, and doctor notes.
  • Emergency Contacts: Names and phone numbers for your family or loved ones so someone can call them quickly.
  • Your Location (GPS): Used only when you tap "Find Help" so you can read your exact address or cross streets to a 911 dispatcher. We never track where you go or save your location history.
  • No Advertising Networks: We do not sell your personal details, share your health habits, or use data brokers. Period.

3. Who Can See Your Emergency Card?

The wristband is made to be read easily by paramedics, nurses, or good Samaritans when you cannot speak. If someone holds their phone right up to your band, your card opens up on their screen. Treat your wristband just like a medical card in your wallet—do not put anything on it that you would not want an emergency responder to see.

4. Account Sync and the Signed-In Wearer Copy

The band copy is the public fragment: the card is built from what is on the chip, and the tap page does not look the profile up. If you sign in and account sync is on, a signed-in wearer copy is also stored in Supabase for your account only, so another phone you sign in on can load it. That copy is not what a stranger's tap reads, and editing it does not rewrite the band. If account sync is off, nothing is stored with us. You can erase the signed-in wearer copy in the app at any time. Storing it is not a HIPAA certification, and RedMed is not a covered entity (see Security below).

Security

The Quick Takeaway

Your profile stays on your phone (Keychain) and on the band if you write one. The band copy is the public fragment. A signed-in wearer copy in Supabase exists only when account sync is on. The tap page does not look the profile up. This is not a HIPAA certification.

What Is Stored Where

When someone taps your band, nothing is fetched from us: the card is built from what is on the chip (the part of the link after the # sign, which your phone's browser reads but never sends to a server). The signed-in wearer copy, when account sync is on, is an additional copy in Supabase for your account. It is not what a stranger's tap reads. Packing on the chip is obfuscation, not a private vault, so only put on the band what you are comfortable with a responder seeing.

HIPAA — not a covered entity

RedMed is not a covered entity and is not a business associate for your RedMed profile. We do not treat patients, process claims, or run an electronic health record. A signed-in wearer copy in Supabase is not a HIPAA certification and not a hospital Security Rule program, and we do not market RedMed as HIPAA-certified or HIPAA-aligned. If EMS or hospital staff later enter what they read into their own systems, that organization's HIPAA rules may apply to its copy. This page is not a HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices.

How Your Band Works

The Quick Takeaway

No batteries. No charging cables. Waterproof. It works using the exact same tap technology as Apple Pay or a tap-to-pay credit card.

No Battery, No Tracking

Your RedMed band has no battery inside. It gets a tiny burst of power from the phone tapping it. That means you never have to charge it, it can't die during an emergency, and it cannot track your steps, heart rate, or location in the background.

To scan it, someone simply taps the top of their phone within 1 to 2 inches of the band. Casual passersby walking past you cannot read your band.
